Privacy

Last updated: 6 November 2026

Little Stories is a personal journal, so here is plainly what happens to what you write in it. It's a small project run by Jeremie Roberrini.

What the app keeps

  • What you enter: people, stories, tags, dates, links, notes about yourself and photos.
  • If you sign in: your email address, and with Google your name and profile picture. Google is used to sign you in and for nothing else.

Where it's kept

  • Without an account: everything stays in your browser, on your device. Clearing your browser's data erases it.
  • With an account: your journal and photos are stored with Supabase in Ireland. The app is hosted on Vercel, and sign-in emails are sent by Resend.

Who can see it

The app only lets each account read and write its own journal, and photos sit in private storage. There's no feed and no sharing. Your journal is not end-to-end encrypted, though, so the person running the service could technically reach the stored data. That access isn't used, except if you ask for help with your own account.

Your journal is never sold or used for advertising, and the app has no ads and no analytics.

Links you share

When you paste a link into Shared things, its address goes to the app's server, which fetches a title and picture from that site. The picture then loads from that site when you view it.

Your choices

  • You can edit or delete anything you've written at any time, and download a copy from the You tab.
  • To delete your account and everything in it, email contact@roberrini.com.
